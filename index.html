What is your company going to make? Please describe your product and what it does or will do.
We've built an AI-native platform that reduces complex battery simulations from 7-8 days to under 30 minutes witnessed by e-TRNL energy, a battery company. The AI agent plans, builds, and runs scientific simulations from plain text. It converts a text prompt into an editable flowchart and clarifies constraints with the researcher before running the physical model on our compute. Researchers can instantly run parameter sweeps and visualize results, condensing weeks of complex scientific modeling into minutes of prompting so researchers can focus purely on the science.

Parametric 3D web editor for e-TRNL unit cell

A patent-faithful 3D mesh of the e-TRNL 3DEA (Three-Dimensional Electrode Architecture) battery unit cell. This is a lithium-ion cell geometry where cathode material is formed into vertical cylindrical pins embedded in an anode matrix, inspired by an origami/pin-array patent.

Final geometry parameters (as-built):

  • hex_R = 1.95 mm — flat-top hexagonal outer envelope
  • H = 4.0 mm — total cell height
  • d_pin = 0.30 mm (300 µm) — cathode pin diameter (LFP)
  • t_sep = 0.025 mm (25 µm) — separator shell thickness (Al₂O₃ ceramic)
  • t_w = 0.025 mm (25 µm) — anode matrix wall between separator shells
  • 61 cathode pins in a 4-ring HCP lattice

Built using brainstorming skill → design decisions → full Three.js implementation in a single index.html. Agent went from requirement clarification to shipped product in one session.

Why did you pick this idea to work on? Do you have domain expertise in this area? How do you know people need what you're making?
We want to build a faster research ecosystem where the focus is science, not labor. We picked this idea because we've lived it - watching researchers lose weeks wrestling with clunky simulation tools, writing boilerplate scripts, and stitching together workflows that should just work, and we saw this pattern repeat across labs, startups, and enterprises. And with current emerging AI models, we've observed it's possible to do now what wasn't possible even 6 months ago. Yes, we both have domain expertise in our field. I have lived the exact problem building ProtonCraft's electrocatalyst, and Kamal has scaled two startups as a founding AI engineer - he has also built simulations and has a keen interest in the space. Talking to customers like e-TRNL and Indi, we realised they not only need specific software licenses but also need a specialist whose only work is to translate science into software GUI.
Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don't?
Incumbents like COMSOL, ASPEN, ANSYS, and SimScale are clusters of physics solvers wrapped in black boxes with decades-long update cycles. New AI-native players like Godela (YC), PhysicsX are interesting, but they operate in mostly mechanical engineering - energy R&D requires domain-specific models for electrochemistry, thermal dynamics, and material science that these tools handle poorly. Incumbents are black boxes with GUI. Researchers can't see the physics, can't swap solvers, can't afford the licence. 80% of better open-source solvers sit unused because integration took weeks - until now. AI is finally good enough to orchestrate domain-specific simulations correctly. We're building at exactly that inflection point - modular, transparent, pay-per-use. Starting with energy R&D where wrong answers cost years.
